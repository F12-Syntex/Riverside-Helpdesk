#### How to use this guide

Each route has its own page. Work down the page in order: what the route is, when to route here, when not to, how to action it, and local detail. This is a care navigation tool, not a clinical assessment tool. Reception staff signpost against practice-agreed protocols using the patient’s own words. Nobody at reception makes a clinical judgement.

#### The rule that overrides every other rule

When you are not sure, route upward. The duty doctor is always the correct destination for anything you cannot confidently place. A task sent to the wrong clinician costs a few minutes. A missed escalation does not.

## SECTION A — EMERGENCY AND URGENT

### 999 — Ambulance

Immediate life or limb threat. This is not a triage decision and does not need a clinician's permission. Dial 999 first, then tell the duty doctor.

#### Route here when

- Chest pain that is central, crushing, or radiating to jaw or arm, especially with sweating, nausea, or breathlessness
- Stroke signs — face droop, arm weakness, slurred or confused speech, sudden loss of vision or balance (FAST)
- Severe difficulty breathing, unable to speak in full sentences, blue lips
- Anaphylaxis — swelling of lips, tongue or throat, widespread rash with breathing difficulty, collapse after a sting, food or medicine
- Unresponsive, fitting for more than five minutes, or not rousable
- Heavy bleeding that does not stop with pressure
- Signs of sepsis — very unwell, mottled or grey skin, not passing urine, rapid breathing, new confusion
- Suspected overdose, or any act of self-harm that has already happened and is causing physical harm

#### Do not route here

- Symptoms the patient has had for days and is now asking about — this is duty doctor, not 999
- Anxiety about symptoms without any of the above features

#### How to action

- Dial 999 from the desk phone. Stay on the line and give the practice address: Theydon Road Health Centre, 14 Urban Hive, Theydon Road, E5 9BQ.
- If the patient is in the building, press the red button at the same time.
- If the patient is on the phone, keep them on the line, take a call-back number, and do not hang up until the ambulance is arranged.
- Tell the duty doctor immediately afterwards and record what happened in the patient record.

#### If you are unsure

If any part of you is unsure whether it is a 999, treat it as a 999 and tell the duty doctor. Nobody will be criticised for calling an ambulance that turned out not to be needed.

SECTION A — EMERGENCY AND URGENT

### Red button — In-practice alarm

The red button in the top right-hand corner of the desk. It alerts everyone in the building, including doctors, that help is needed at reception now.

#### Route here when

- A patient collapses, faints, or becomes acutely unwell in the waiting room or at the desk
- A patient is behaving in a way that makes you feel unsafe — aggression, threats, refusing to leave
- A patient in the building describes symptoms that would be a 999 call
- Any situation where you need a clinician physically present immediately

#### Do not route here

- Phone calls — the red button brings staff to reception, it does nothing for a caller. Use the duty doctor interrupt route instead.
- A patient who is upset or crying but safe — offer a private space and tell the duty doctor

#### How to action

- Press the button. Do not leave the desk to find someone first.
- If it is a medical emergency, dial 999 as well — the two are not alternatives.
- Once help arrives, hand over what you saw and heard, in the patient's own words where possible.

#### If you are unsure

When in doubt, press it. Raising the alarm early is always the right call and there is no penalty for a false alarm.

SECTION A — EMERGENCY AND URGENT

### Duty doctor interrupt

Breaking into the duty doctor's clinic for a patient who cannot wait for a call-back. Used for phone calls and online requests where the patient is deteriorating but it is not yet a 999.

#### Route here when

- Caller sounds breathless, confused, or is struggling to complete sentences
- A parent describing a child who is floppy, not feeding, not responding normally, or has a rash that does not fade under a glass
- New severe pain of sudden onset — worst headache ever, sudden abdominal pain, testicular pain
- Pregnant patient with bleeding, severe abdominal pain, reduced fetal movements, or severe headache
- Anyone describing thoughts of ending their life, or a plan to harm themselves or someone else
- A patient whose symptoms are getting visibly worse while you are talking to them

#### Do not route here

- Symptoms that are stable and have been present for days — book the duty list instead
- Requests for results, medication, or letters, however insistent

#### How to action

- Take a call-back number before doing anything else.
- Walk to the duty doctor rather than sending a task. Tasks are not read in real time.
- Record what you were told in the patient's own words, and the time you escalated.
- Do not end the call until you have been told what to tell the patient.

#### If you are unsure

Interrupting is always safer than waiting. If the doctor decides it could have waited, that is a better outcome than a delayed deterioration.

SECTION A — EMERGENCY AND URGENT

### A&E — self-transport

Urgent problems needing hospital assessment today where the patient is stable enough to travel themselves or with a family member. Homerton Hospital A&E is the local department.

#### Route here when

- Suspected fracture or dislocation after a fall or injury
- Wounds needing stitching or a deep cut that will not stop bleeding with pressure
- Head injury with vomiting, drowsiness, or loss of consciousness at the time
- Burns beyond a small superficial area
- Acute severe pain that the practice cannot assess safely today

#### Do not route here

- Anything from the 999 list — those patients must not drive or be driven
- Longstanding problems the patient is frustrated about waiting for
- Minor illness — sore throats, coughs, rashes. These go to Pharmacy First or the duty list

#### How to action

- Advise the patient to attend and tell the duty doctor what you have advised.
- Record the advice given in the patient record.
- Never arrange transport yourself — if the patient cannot get there safely, that is a 999.

#### Local detail

Under the GP contract, if a patient attends A&E for a non-emergency during our opening hours the practice is penalised. When an A&E letter arrives for something we could have handled, it is coded as an A&E attendance during surgery hours and the patient is contacted to explain what they can do next time. This is a follow-up task, not something to raise with the patient in the moment.

#### If you are unsure

If you cannot tell whether the patient is safe to travel, escalate to the duty doctor before advising A&E.

SECTION A — EMERGENCY AND URGENT

### Eye emergency

Eye problems that cannot be managed by a community optician and need same-day specialist assessment.

#### Route here when

- Sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes
- Chemical splash to the eye — advise immediate irrigation while arranging
- Penetrating injury, or a foreign body that cannot be flushed out
- Severe eye pain with vomiting, halos around lights, or a hard red eye
- Sudden onset of a curtain or shadow across the vision

#### Do not route here

- Styes, gritty eyes, conjunctivitis, mild watering or discharge — these go to the minor eye service at a community optician
- Gradual blurring over weeks — routine optician or GP

#### How to action

- Tell the duty doctor. Do not advise the patient to attend anywhere until the duty doctor has confirmed the destination.
- For chemical injury, advise the patient to start rinsing the eye with clean water immediately while you are speaking to them.

#### Local detail

Minor eye conditions are seen locally by Rose Opticians, 27 Lower Clapton Road, E5 0NS, 020 8985 1971. If the patient cannot be seen within 24 hours, tell the GP.

The minor eye service covers styes, red or sore eyes, mild trauma or a scratch to the surface of the eye, suspected foreign body, recent flashes and floaters, recent double vision, and significant discharge or watering.

#### If you are unsure

Sudden change plus pain, or sudden change plus vision loss, is always a duty doctor conversation and never a routine optician booking.

## SECTION B — PRACTICE CLINICIANS

### Duty / on-call GP

Same-day clinical assessment by a doctor. This is the practice's main destination for acute problems that are not emergencies, and the default for anything you cannot confidently place elsewhere.

#### Route here when

- New symptoms that have come on over hours or days and the patient is unwell with them
- Fever in an unwell patient, particularly the very young, frail, or immunosuppressed
- Worsening of a known long-term condition — asthma or COPD with more breathlessness, heart failure with new swelling, diabetes with extreme readings
- Suspected infection needing examination — chest, abdomen, urine, skin, or a hot swollen joint
- Pain that is new, severe, or escalating
- A patient assessed recently, calling back no better or worse
- Any presentation that worries you with no clear alternative destination

#### Do not route here

- Administrative requests — letters, forms, results chasing, registration
- Minor illness that falls squarely inside a Pharmacy First pathway with no complicating features
- MSK pain in an adult with no red flags — this goes to the FCP
- Medication requests — these go to the pharmacy team
- Anything on the 999 or interrupt list — those do not wait for a call-back

#### How to action

- Decide whether an examination is likely to be needed: no means a telephone consultation with the on-duty doctor, yes means a face-to-face slot.
- Book the earliest available slot. Appointments, then Find Slot, then select the clinician and slot type; right-click the slot and book the current patient.
- Record the reason in the patient's own words, not clinical language.
- Take a contact number and best times to call. For online consultations, add the phone number as a comment on the slot.
- Acknowledge the request through Screen Messages.
- If the patient deteriorates while waiting, escalate as a duty doctor interrupt.

#### Telephone or face-to-face

- The test is whether the doctor will need to look at, listen to, or feel something. If yes or probably, book face-to-face.
- Face-to-face: rashes, lumps, swelling, wounds, ear or throat problems, chest or abdominal pain, anything the patient wants shown
- Telephone: medication side effects, review of a previous consultation, results, advice on a known condition
- If in doubt, book face-to-face. Converting that to a phone call is far easier than the reverse.

#### Capacity and what to do when the list is full

- A full list does not change the clinical need. If the patient meets the criteria above, speak to the duty doctor rather than offering a routine appointment next week.
- Enhanced Access hub slots suit one-off problems only and are not a substitute for duty assessment of an unwell patient.
- Never tell a patient to call back tomorrow to manage capacity. Escalate instead.

#### Local detail

Patients should be asked to use the online form for most requests; the exception is an emergency, where they can call and we arrange an urgent call with the doctor. Submissions arrive in the triage form in Accurx and are assessed there before booking.

Online requests may not be seen for up to two working days, so they must never be the route for an urgent problem.

#### If you are unsure

The duty list is the correct place for anything you cannot confidently place elsewhere. Uncertainty routes upward, never sideways, and never into a longer wait.

SECTION B — PRACTICE CLINICIANS

### GP routine appointment

A booked telephone or face-to-face appointment with a doctor for a problem that is not urgent and does not need same-day assessment.

#### Route here when

- Ongoing symptoms the patient wants reviewed, which are stable and not worsening
- Follow-up after a hospital letter, discharge summary, or a previous consultation
- Discussion of test results where the doctor has asked for an appointment rather than a call
- Medication questions that need a doctor rather than the pharmacy team — for example stopping a long-term medicine, or a change the pharmacy team has referred on
- Long-term condition concerns that are not part of a nurse-led review
- Second opinion or review of a plan the patient is unhappy with
- Requests for a specific doctor where continuity matters — ongoing mental health, complex or palliative patients, safeguarding-flagged families

#### Do not route here

- Anything the patient describes as getting worse, or that has changed since it started — reassess for the duty list
- New patients who have not yet had their new patient check
- Reviews that belong to the nursing team — asthma, diabetes, at-risk, mental health, learning disability
- Requests that are purely administrative — these are a doctor task, not an appointment
- MSK problems in adults with no red flags — the FCP is faster and can order imaging directly

#### How to action

- Book the next suitable slot: Appointments, then Find Slot, then select the clinician and slot type.
- Record the reason for the appointment so the doctor can prepare.
- Where a doctor has sent a task asking us to book a patient in, set the task type as instructed — tasks from Dr Rishi are changed to 1Telephone Review.
- For a future appointment that cannot be booked yet, go to the patient's profile, then Workflow, then Add task, write the note and mark it urgent.
- For patients who cannot attend in core hours, offer an Enhanced Access appointment at a PCN hub.
- Acknowledge the request through Screen Messages once booked.

#### Continuity — when the same doctor matters

Most routine problems can be seen by any available doctor. Some should not be, and offering the earliest slot in those cases is a false economy because the patient has to repeat their history.

- Ongoing mental health, where retelling the story is itself harmful
- Complex patients with several interacting conditions
- Palliative and end of life patients and their families
- Anything with a safeguarding flag on the record
- A patient who has been seen twice already for the same unresolved problem

#### Enhanced Access hub appointments

Hackney Downs PCN runs early morning, evening and Saturday appointments across three sites, open to all patients registered at any PCN practice. Book through us in the usual way and mention that an Enhanced Access appointment is wanted.

- Mornings 07:00–08:00 at Nightingale Practice, GP most days and nurse on Thursdays
- Evenings 18:30–20:00 at Fountayne Road on Monday, Tuesday and Friday, and Healy Medical Centre on Wednesday and Thursday
- Saturdays 09:00–17:00 at Nightingale Practice with GP, nurse, phlebotomist and pharmacist
- Best for one-off problems that will not need follow-up — patients are asked to follow up with us
- The clinician can see our records and the consultation appears in ours
- Any test requests or referrals raised at a hub come back to us to action

#### On the day

- Late arrivals can be checked in directly in the booked area.
- If a patient misses their call, they can be called back within clinic hours; outside that they need to redo the online form.
- Most requests, including registration forms and registration confirmation, can be signposted to the online consultation form. Paid requests such as registration confirmation letters and private letters still follow the reports and payments process.

#### Local detail

The practice is open Monday to Friday and closed at weekends. Enhanced Access is the only weekend option.

New patients cannot be booked a routine appointment until they have had their new patient check.

#### If you are unsure

If the patient is asking for the soonest possible appointment because they feel unwell now, that is a duty list question, not a routine booking. The words to listen for are today, worse, and can't wait.

SECTION B — PRACTICE CLINICIANS

### Doctor task

A written task to a doctor where no appointment is needed yet. The doctor decides what happens next, including which onward referral is appropriate.

#### Route here when

- Requests for a referral of any kind — the doctor chooses the service, speciality and priority
- Test results that have returned and need review
- Fit note requests requiring a clinical decision
- Queries from a patient about advice a doctor gave previously
- Anything that would previously have been signposted to an outside service, where the right service is a clinical judgement

#### Do not route here

- Anything time-critical — tasks are not read in real time
- Medication requests, which go to the pharmacy team
- Straightforward bookings where a slot exists

#### How to action

- Write the task in the patient's own words. Do not summarise symptoms into your own clinical-sounding phrasing.
- Include what the patient is asking for, how long it has been going on, and a contact number.
- Mark the task to the named doctor if the patient has an existing relationship with them, otherwise to the duty doctor.

#### Local detail

Referral decisions belong to the doctor. Reception does not choose a speciality, a clinic type, or a priority.

Once the doctor has decided, the referral itself is prepared and sent by the referrals workflow, either through e-RS or by email depending on the service.

#### If you are unsure

If a patient is pressing for a specific referral they have researched themselves, that still goes to the doctor as a task rather than being actioned directly.

SECTION B — PRACTICE CLINICIANS

### Practice nurse

The nursing team covers both nursing and healthcare assistant work at this practice. Clinics run on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays.

#### Route here when

- Long-term condition reviews — asthma, diabetes, at-risk (non-diabetic hyperglycaemia), mental health, learning disability
- Cervical screening, swabs, ear checks
- Dressings and wound care for patients who can attend the surgery
- Travel vaccinations and childhood immunisations
- Blood tests, blood pressure checks, ECGs
- NHS Health Checks and new patient health checks
- HRT reviews

#### Do not route here

- Blood tests for anyone under 16 — the practice is not equipped for paediatric phlebotomy
- Acute illness needing diagnosis — that is the duty list
- Contraceptive pill requests, which go to the pharmacy team

#### How to action

- Check whether the review needs bloods first. Diabetes, at-risk, mental health, learning disability, NHS Health Checks and new patient checks all need a blood test booked beforehand.
- Blood tests for a morning nurse appointment must be taken before 13:00.
- For HRT, the nurse needs a BMI and blood pressure reading recorded.
- Travel vaccinations need six weeks' notice — patients asking with less time should be told at the point of booking.

#### Local detail

HPV vaccination can only be given by our nurse to patients aged 24 and under. Patients aged 25 and over must access it independently.

Nurses are only trained to take blood from patients aged 16 and over.

Saturday phlebotomy is available at the Nightingale Practice Enhanced Access hub for patients who cannot attend in the week.

#### If you are unsure

If you cannot tell whether a review needs bloods first, book the bloods. An unnecessary blood test is a smaller problem than a wasted review appointment.

SECTION B — PRACTICE CLINICIANS

### Diabetes review

Structured diabetes care, run by the nursing team. Any of our practice nurses can carry out a diabetes check; we also have a specialist diabetes nurse for more complex reviews and newly diagnosed patients. Kept as a separate route because of the sequencing and the appointment length.

#### Route here when

- Annual or interval diabetes review for a patient on the diabetes register
- Newly diagnosed diabetic patients needing their first review
- Patients asking about a diabetes blood test — book a nurse appointment, reason recorded as diabetes blood test
- At-risk reviews for non-diabetic hyperglycaemia
- Patients recalled after an abnormal HbA1c

#### Do not route here

- A diabetic patient who feels unwell today — vomiting, drowsiness, extreme readings, or an infection that is not settling. Duty doctor.
- Foot problems with broken skin, discolouration, or a wound not healing — doctor task, urgently
- Eye symptoms in a diabetic patient — a separate clinical question for the doctor
- Requests to change insulin doses — pharmacy team, not a review booking

#### How to action

- Book the blood test first, then the review.
- Newly diagnosed patients get a double appointment.
- Newly diagnosed patients are also sent to the diabetes education programme.
- Bloods for a morning nurse appointment must be taken before 13:00.
- Where the patient is complex, newly diagnosed, or struggling with control, book with the specialist diabetes nurse rather than the general nurse list.

#### Who sees the patient

- Any practice nurse can do a routine diabetes check for a stable patient on the register
- The specialist diabetes nurse takes newly diagnosed patients, poor control, and anything a general check cannot settle
- Bloods can be taken by any of the nursing team, and on Saturdays by the phlebotomist at the Nightingale hub
- If the patient asks for the diabetes nurse, book the diabetes nurse — do not offer the next general slot

#### Sequencing

- Diabetes review is a two-appointment process. Booking them the wrong way round wastes both.
- Blood results usually take about five working days to come back
- If a patient books a review with no recent bloods, add the blood test rather than cancelling the review
- The bloods-first rule also applies to at-risk, mental health and LD reviews, NHS Health Checks and new patient checks

#### Local detail

The practice manages blood levels and blood testing. Diabetic eye screening is a separate national programme and is not booked as a practice appointment. Patients asking for a diabetes blood test do not need to see a doctor first.

#### If you are unsure

A diabetic patient who feels unwell today is a duty doctor question, not a review booking, however overdue their review is.

SECTION B — PRACTICE CLINICIANS

### First Contact Physiotherapist (FCP)

An advanced physiotherapist working inside the practice who assesses, diagnoses and manages musculoskeletal problems without the patient seeing a GP first. Provided across Hackney Downs PCN by the Homerton FCP service. Around one in five GP appointments are for joint, muscle or nerve pain, so this is one of the highest-volume routes on this list.

#### Route here when

- Neck and back pain, including stiffness, spasm, and pain that has come on with no clear cause
- Sciatica and other nerve pain radiating into an arm or leg, including pins and needles or numbness in a limb
- Shoulder pain, frozen shoulder, rotator cuff problems, pain on lifting overhead
- Elbow, wrist and hand pain, including tennis elbow, carpal tunnel symptoms, and thumb base pain
- Hip, knee, ankle and foot pain, including plantar heel pain and Achilles problems
- Muscle and soft tissue pain, strains, and tendon problems
- Flare-ups of a longstanding MSK problem the patient has had before
- Osteoarthritis pain and stiffness where the patient wants help managing it
- Weakness, giving way, or loss of function after a minor injury
- Patients asking for a physiotherapy referral for a new MSK problem — the FCP is the assessment, not a step before it

#### Do not route here

- Anyone under 16 — FCPs are trained and equipped for adults only. Route to a doctor.
- Suspected fracture or dislocation, or significant recent trauma — A&E
- Inability to weight-bear at all after an injury — A&E
- A joint that is hot, swollen and red, especially with fever — duty doctor the same day
- Back pain with loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the saddle area, or new weakness in both legs — duty doctor interrupt, immediately
- Back pain with unexplained weight loss, night sweats, or a history of cancer — duty doctor
- Pain that is part of a wider illness where the patient is systemically unwell
- Chest pain the patient is describing as muscular — this is a duty doctor question until a doctor says otherwise
- Post-operative problems, wound issues, or complications after orthopaedic surgery — doctor task
- A hospital has already told the patient they need physiotherapy — doctor task, so the referral goes to the right service

#### How to action

- Book through Find slot, then Find across organisation slot, then Hackney Downs PCN, then FCP new patient.
- Screen for the exclusions above before booking. Ask how it started, whether there was an injury, and whether anything has changed in bladder, bowel or leg strength.
- Appointments may be offered at another PCN practice site. The FCP can see our records and the consultation appears in ours.
- Follow-up appointments are not booked directly — send a task to the FCP group on EMIS saying the patient needs a follow-up.
- Record the reason in the patient's own words, including which body part and how long.

#### Questions to ask before booking

These are not clinical judgements. They are the questions the FCP service expects reception to ask, and the answers decide whether the FCP slot is the right one.

- Which part of the body, and how long has it been going on?
- Did it start with an injury or accident? If so, what happened?
- Can you put weight on it or use it at all?
- Any numbness or weakness, and if so where?
- For back pain: any change in bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the saddle area?
- Are you unwell in yourself — fever, night sweats, weight loss?
- How old is the patient?

#### What the FCP can do

Worth knowing so you can explain why the FCP is not a lesser option than a GP appointment. Patients sometimes push back on being offered a physiotherapist.

- Assess and diagnose the problem in the first appointment
- Refer directly for imaging — X-ray, MRI or ultrasound
- Refer for blood tests
- Ask for medication to be prescribed
- Refer onward to secondary care, for example orthopaedics or rheumatology
- Provide exercise and self-management advice and arrange further physiotherapy

#### What to tell the patient

- The FCP is a senior physiotherapist with advanced training, not a step down from seeing a doctor
- They usually get seen sooner and can arrange scans directly, so it is quicker than a GP referral
- They are trained to spot anything serious and will escalate to a GP if needed
- If the patient still wants a GP, book the GP — do not argue them into an FCP slot

#### Local detail

FCPs are trained to identify red flag symptoms and will escalate, but that does not remove the screening step at reception. The exclusions above exist so that patients who need a doctor or A&E are not delayed by a physiotherapy appointment.

Extended Scope Physiotherapy sits within the same physiotherapy service and is arranged by a clinician, not booked at reception.

#### If you are unsure

If the MSK problem started with an accident in the last few days, ask about the mechanism before booking. Falls from height, direct blows, and inability to weight-bear need a doctor or A&E, not an FCP slot.

## SECTION C — PHARMACY

### Practice pharmacy team

The internal pharmacy team. Every medication-related request goes here, without exception.

#### Route here when

- Repeat prescription requests and queries about them
- Questions about what a medication is for, dosing, or side effects
- Requests to change quantity, formulation, or pharmacy nomination
- Contraceptive pill requests and continuation
- Queries from a community pharmacist about one of our prescriptions
- Discharge medication queries after a hospital stay

#### Do not route here

- New symptoms that happen to involve medication — if the patient is asking to be seen about a problem, that is a clinical route
- Suspected adverse reaction where the patient is unwell now — duty doctor

#### How to action

- Send to the pharmacy team as a task. Allow about three working days.
- Medication cannot be issued without a prescription request. This applies particularly to controlled drugs.
- When a community pharmacy calls, take the detail and pass it on — do not agree to issue anything at the desk.

#### Local detail

PCN pharmacists can also help with medication reviews, repeat contraception and HRT, and reviews of some long-term conditions such as asthma, hypertension and depression.

A pharmacist is available at the Friday evening and Saturday Enhanced Access hub sessions.

#### If you are unsure

If it involves a medicine, it goes to the pharmacy team. If the patient also needs assessing, it goes to the pharmacy team and a clinical route.

SECTION C — PHARMACY

### Pharmacy First referral

An electronic referral to a community pharmacist who can assess and treat, not just advise. Sent through Local Services on EMIS or PharmRefer. Referring rather than signposting transfers clinical responsibility to the pharmacy and means they follow the patient up.

#### Route here when

- Uncomplicated urinary tract infection — women aged 16 to 64
- Shingles — 18 and over
- Impetigo — 1 year and over
- Infected insect bites — 1 year and over
- Sinusitis — 12 and over
- Sore throat — 5 and over
- Acute otitis media (earache) — 1 to 17
- Minor illness more broadly: cough, cold, diarrhoea, constipation, mouth ulcers, rashes, hay fever, sprains and strains, blocked ear or wax, red or sticky eye, teething, scabies, athlete's foot, wound dressings

#### Do not route here

- Anyone outside the age range for that pathway — the pharmacist cannot treat and it stays with us
- Patients who are systemically unwell, not just locally unwell
- Pregnant patients with a UTI
- Recurrent presentations of the same problem that has already failed pharmacy treatment
- Suspected shingles affecting the eye or the tip of the nose — duty doctor

#### How to action

- Refer electronically through Local Services on EMIS or PharmRefer rather than telling the patient to walk in. The system returns nearby participating pharmacies.
- Let the patient choose the pharmacy. They have a right to use any participating pharmacy, not just the nearest.
- The pharmacy sends a consultation notification back to us the same or next working day, which is filed to the record.

#### Local detail

Hackney Pharmacy and Travel Clinic, 15a Urban Hive, Theydon Road, E5 9BQ, 020 8806 6215. In the same building as the surgery. Monday to Friday 09:00 to 19:00, Saturday 11:00 to 16:00, closed Sunday. Offers Pharmacy First.

AsvaCare Pharmacy, 97 Upper Clapton Road, E5 9BU, 020 8806 5140. Monday to Friday 09:00 to 19:00, Saturday 10:00 to 15:00, closed Sunday.

F. A. Strange, 185 Lower Clapton Road, E5 8EQ, 020 8510 0829. Open Saturdays.

Almost all pharmacies across City and Hackney are signed up, so patients who work outside the area can be referred to a pharmacy near their workplace.

#### If you are unsure

The age range is the gateway. If the patient falls outside it, the pathway is closed to them and the request stays with us — do not refer and hope.

## SECTION D — COMMUNITY SERVICES

### Midwife — antenatal self-referral

All pregnancy care is arranged directly with the midwifery service. Patients do not need to see a GP first to book antenatal care.

#### Route here when

- Newly pregnant patient asking how to start antenatal care
- Questions about booking the 12-week scan or other scans
- Patient asking to be referred to a midwife

#### Do not route here

- Pregnant patient with bleeding, severe pain, reduced fetal movements, or severe headache — duty doctor interrupt
- Questions about medication safety in pregnancy — pharmacy team or doctor task
- Patients wanting to discuss options after an unplanned pregnancy — doctor task

#### How to action

- Give the patient the Homerton antenatal self-referral form: homerton.nhs.uk/antenatal-self-referral-form
- Explain that the midwife will guide them on all the scans they need, including the 12-week scan.
- Record that the patient was signposted, so we know the pregnancy has been notified.

#### If you are unsure

Any pregnant patient describing a physical symptom rather than asking an administrative question should be treated as a clinical route, not a self-referral signpost.

SECTION D — COMMUNITY SERVICES

### District Nurse / Adult Community Nursing

Nursing care delivered in the patient's home for people who cannot attend the surgery.

#### Route here when

- Housebound patients needing wound care, dressings, or leg ulcer management
- Housebound patients needing injections, catheter care, or blood tests at home
- Palliative and end of life care at home
- Continence assessment and pressure area care

#### Do not route here

- Patients who can physically attend the surgery, even with difficulty — practice nurse
- Patients wanting help with shopping, benefits or isolation — social prescriber
- Urgent deterioration in a housebound patient — duty doctor

#### How to action

- The referral is made on the RP ACN 2022 form.
- Supporting documents are ordered latest to earliest, printed to a single PDF, and emailed with the referral.
- The doctor confirms the clinical need before the referral goes.
- Being housebound is the gateway. Ask directly whether the patient can get to the surgery at all, and record the answer.

#### Local detail

Community services in City and Hackney, including district nursing, are organised by Neighbourhood, so our patients are covered by the local team rather than a borough-wide service.

#### If you are unsure

If it is unclear whether the patient is genuinely housebound, raise it as a doctor task rather than sending the referral.

SECTION D — COMMUNITY SERVICES

### Social prescriber

A PCN role connecting patients to community groups, activities and statutory services for practical, social and emotional support. Not a clinical appointment.

#### Route here when

- Money worries, debt, benefits applications, help filling in forms
- Housing problems and difficulties with council services
- Loneliness and social isolation
- Patients who are managing but would benefit from community activity, groups, or exercise
- Carers needing support in their own right

#### Do not route here

- Patients in mental health crisis or expressing thoughts of self-harm — duty doctor, immediately
- Patients needing coordination of care packages, transport or social services — care coordinator
- Requests for a medical report to support a benefits claim — this is an administrative and clinical task, not social prescribing

#### How to action

- Book through reception in the usual way, or email nelondonicb.hackneydownssp@nhs.net through Accurx: down arrow next to Chat, then Message professional.
- Explain briefly what the patient needs help with.
- Ask the patient's permission before sending — this is a support service, not an automatic referral.

#### Local detail

The PCN also has a Health and Wellbeing Coach for lifestyle change and self-management of conditions such as diabetes, weight and chronic pain, and a Voluntary Sector Care Coordinator who helps with transport, social services and care packages. Both are booked through reception.

The PCN Wellbeing Practitioner works longer term with patients facing multiple complex physical, mental health and social problems. This is arranged via the GP rather than booked directly.

#### If you are unsure

If the patient's difficulty is practical and social, social prescribing is right. If they are distressed rather than stuck, involve a clinician first.

## APPLIES TO EVERY ROUTE

#### Flags and hard gates

#### Flags that modify a route, not replace it

These do not change where the request goes. They change what else happens alongside it, and they are recorded either way.

- Safeguarding — any concern about a child or adult at risk goes to the safeguarding lead as well as the clinical route. Never delay the clinical route while you decide.
- Domestic abuse — never leave a message, never send correspondence to the home address, and check whether it is safe to call back before agreeing a time.
- Interpreter needed — book Language Line and note the language on the appointment so the clinician knows before the call.
- Carer status — record it, and consider whether the carer themselves needs a route of their own.
- Learning disability or reasonable adjustments — longer appointments, quieter times, a named contact, or written follow-up.
- Digital exclusion — do not signpost a self-referral link to a patient who cannot use it. Arrange the route for them instead.

#### Hard gates

These are absolute. They are not clinical judgements and reception does not need permission to apply them.
